# Module 7: Ethics Governance and Organizational Risk

## Module Overview

This module addresses the risks that emerge when applied AI enters real organizations. Students learn how ethical, legal, and organizational factors affect success. The focus stays on prevention, responsibility, and sound judgment.

## Module Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students can:

1. Identify ethical and legal risks in applied AI systems.
2. Assess organizational failure patterns related to applied AI.
3. Propose mitigation strategies that reduce risk and increase trust.

## Lesson 1: Ethical and Legal Considerations

### Lesson Objective
This lesson supports the objective of identifying ethical and legal risks in applied AI systems.

### Lesson Overview
Applied AI affects people and decisions. This lesson introduces ethical and legal considerations that shape acceptable use.

### Core Content
Ethical risk appears when systems affect fairness, access, or accountability. Bias, exclusion, and opacity undermine trust.

Legal risk depends on data type and use. Privacy laws, employment rules, and industry regulations impose limits.

Compliance begins with awareness. Teams identify obligations early and design within them.

Responsible applied AI balances capability with duty.

### Worked Example
A hiring support tool excludes protected attributes and documents decision boundaries.

### Check for Understanding
Students identify ethical and legal risks in short scenarios.

### Required Material
Lesson reading provided in the LMS.

### Optional Enrichment
Regulatory examples provided in the course repository.

## Lesson 2: Organizational Failure Patterns

### Lesson Objective
This lesson supports the objective of assessing organizational failure patterns related to applied AI.

### Lesson Overview
Many applied AI failures stem from organizational issues rather than technical ones. This lesson examines those patterns.

### Core Content
Failures follow predictable paths. Leadership expects certainty. Teams lack ownership. Incentives reward speed over care.

Shadow systems emerge when trust erodes. Users bypass tools they do not understand.

Clear roles and expectations reduce risk. Governance supports sustainability.

Successful teams align incentives with responsible use.

### Worked Example
An AI tool fails adoption due to unclear ownership and lack of training. The issue reflects process, not technology.

### Check for Understanding
Students identify organizational risks and suggest corrective actions.

### Required Material
Lesson reading provided in the LMS.

### Optional Enrichment
Organizational case studies provided in the course repository.

## Lesson 3: Mitigation and Guardrails

### Lesson Objective
This lesson supports the objective of proposing mitigation strategies that reduce risk and increase trust.

### Lesson Overview
Guardrails enable safe use. This lesson focuses on practical mitigation strategies.

### Core Content
Mitigation begins with scope. Narrow use reduces exposure.

Human review adds accountability. Audit trails support oversight.

Documentation preserves intent. Training sets expectations.

Effective guardrails balance control and usability.

### Worked Example
A decision support system requires human approval for high impact outputs. Logs capture actions.

### Check for Understanding
Students propose guardrails for a given use case.

### Required Material
Lesson reading provided in the LMS.

### Optional Enrichment
Mitigation templates provided in the course repository.

## Module 7 Assignment: Risk Assessment Memo

### Assignment Overview
Students assess ethical, legal, and organizational risks for an applied AI system. The assignment emphasizes foresight and responsibility.

### Assignment Instructions
Students submit a short memo. The memo identifies key risks, explains their impact, and proposes mitigation strategies. The focus stays on practical action.

### Assessment Criteria
- Ethical and legal risks are clearly identified.
- Organizational risks are accurately assessed.
- Mitigation strategies are appropriate and realistic.
- The analysis reflects responsible judgment.

### Alignment
This assignment assesses Module 7 objectives one and three and Course Learning Outcome five.
