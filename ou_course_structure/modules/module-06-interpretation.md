# Module 6: Interpretation and Communication

## Module Overview

This module focuses on making applied AI output usable and trustworthy. Students learn how to read results, recognize limits, and communicate clearly. The emphasis stays on judgment, explanation, and shared understanding.

## Module Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, students can:

1. Interpret AI output and uncertainty.
2. Identify hallucination and misuse risks.
3. Communicate AI results clearly to non technical stakeholders.

## Lesson 1: Reading AI Output

### Lesson Objective
This lesson supports the objective of interpreting AI output and uncertainty.

### Lesson Overview
AI output requires interpretation. This lesson teaches students how to read results with care and context.

### Core Content
Applied AI output reflects probabilities, patterns, and approximations. It does not provide certainty.

Scores, rankings, and generated text require context. Confidence does not equal correctness.

Uncertainty appears in many forms. Variance, ambiguity, and missing information affect results.

Good practitioners read output critically and compare it to expectations.

### Worked Example
A forecasting model reports a range rather than a single value. The range guides decisions better than a point estimate.

### Check for Understanding
Students interpret sample outputs and explain what they do and do not indicate.

## Lesson 2: Detecting Unreliable Results

### Lesson Objective
This lesson supports the objective of identifying hallucination and misuse risks.

### Lesson Overview
AI systems can produce fluent but incorrect results. This lesson teaches students to detect and manage unreliability.

### Core Content
Hallucination occurs when systems generate plausible but false content. This risk increases when context lacks grounding.

Misuse follows unclear boundaries. Users apply outputs beyond their intended scope.

Detection relies on cross checks, constraints, and human review. Guardrails reduce exposure.

Clear limits protect trust and credibility.

### Worked Example
A document assistant cites sources for retrieved content. Missing citations signal caution.

### Check for Understanding
Students identify warning signs in sample outputs and suggest mitigation steps.

### Required Material
Lesson reading provided in the LMS.

### Optional Enrichment
Reliability patterns and examples provided in the course repository.

## Lesson 3: Communicating Insights Clearly

### Lesson Objective
This lesson supports the objective of communicating AI results clearly to non technical stakeholders.

### Lesson Overview
Communication determines impact. This lesson focuses on translating results into plain language.

### Core Content
Effective communication begins with the question the system addresses. Results follow.

Avoid technical detail. Emphasize implications, limits, and next steps.

Visuals support clarity when used sparingly. Words often suffice.

Clear communication builds trust and enables action.

### Worked Example
A summary report explains what changed, why it matters, and what to do next. Model details remain secondary.

### Check for Understanding
Students rewrite a technical output as a short stakeholder message.

### Required Material
Lesson reading provided in the LMS.

### Optional Enrichment
Communication templates provided in the course repository.

## Module 6 Assignment: Executive Summary Brief

### Assignment Overview
Students translate applied AI results into a clear stakeholder communication. The assignment emphasizes clarity and judgment.

### Assignment Instructions
Students submit a one page executive summary. The summary explains the purpose of the AI system, key results, limits, and recommended actions. The audience is non technical leadership.

### Assessment Criteria
- Results are accurately interpreted.
- Limits and uncertainty are clearly stated.
- Language is clear and accessible.
- Recommendations align with results.

### Alignment
This assignment assesses Module 6 objectives one and three and Course Learning Outcome four.
