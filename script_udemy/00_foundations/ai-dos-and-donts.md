At this point, the risk is not misunderstanding AI—it is misusing it. Because the system is capable, but it follows your direction. So the discipline shifts to you: you treat the system as a collaborator, not an answer machine. You ask, you review, you refine, and you stay engaged. Now this becomes a habit.

You do not accept the first answer. You question it, you compare versions, you improve the prompt, and through that process the output improves because the system responds to how you guide it. Now skepticism matters. The model produces fluent answers, but fluency is not truth. So you verify, you check, and you take responsibility.

Iteration becomes the method. You start with a draft and improve it step by step, because quality emerges through revision, not in one pass. Clarity matters again: if your question is vague, the result is vague; if your question is precise, the result improves. So thinking clearly becomes part of using AI well.

Using AI is not passive—it is active. You shape the interaction, you guide the system, you evaluate the result, and that is what separates effective use from poor use.
