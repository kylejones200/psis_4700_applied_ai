# Week 1 — What Is AI?

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLO) Alignment
- **CLO-A**: Define core AI concepts and distinguish between different AI approaches
- **CLO-B**: Identify opportunities and risks of AI in real-world contexts
- **CLO-C**: Evaluate AI systems critically using appropriate frameworks

---

## Module 1.1: Foundations of AI

### Module Learning Outcomes
- **LO 1.1**: Define AI, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning in plain language [CLO-A]
- **LO 1.2**: Distinguish between symbolic, statistical, and generative AI approaches [CLO-A]
- **LO 1.3**: Explain key historical developments and current capabilities/limits of AI [CLO-A, CLO-C]

### Required Materials & Resources
- **Required Readings**:
  1. Course slides: `webslides/week01_what-is-ai/01-main-lesson.md` (45-60 min)
  2. Reference document: `course_materials/reference/what_is_ai.md` (15 min)
  3. AI Terms Glossary: `course_materials/reference/ai_terms_plain_english.md` - Review sections on AI, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Generative AI, Prompt, Hallucination (20 min)
- **Optional Readings**:
  - Brief history of AI article (e.g., MIT Technology Review, Harvard Business Review)
  - Recent news article about AI applications in your field
- **Videos** (if available):
  - Brief history of AI overview
- **Interactive Activities**:
  - Explore a chatbot tool (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude) - try asking it questions and observe responses
  - Categorize AI examples by approach (worksheet provided)

### Key Vocabulary
- AI, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Symbolic AI, Statistical AI, Generative AI, Prompt, Token, Hallucination

### Learning Activities (Formative - Not Graded)
1. **Self-Study**: Review course slides and reference materials
2. **Interactive Exploration**: Use a chatbot tool to understand how conversational AI works
3. **Practice Exercise**: Complete worksheet categorizing AI examples by approach (symbolic, statistical, generative)

### Estimated Time to Complete
- Reading and review: 60-75 minutes
- Interactive exploration: 30-45 minutes
- Practice exercises: 30 minutes
- **Total: 2-2.5 hours**

## Gradable Activities

### Discussion: AI Definitions and Approaches (25 points)
- **Initial Post** (100 words minimum): Define AI, ML, and DL in your own words. Provide one example of each approach (symbolic, statistical, generative) from your experience or research. 
- **Reply** (75 words minimum): Respond to one peer's post, comparing their examples to your own or asking a clarifying question.
- **Due Dates**:
  - Initial post: End of Week 1 (Sunday 11:59 PM)
  - Reply to peer: 48 hours after initial post deadline
- **Evaluation Criteria**: 
  - Accurate definitions of AI, ML, DL (40%)
  - Clear examples for each approach (30%)
  - Thoughtful engagement with peers in reply (30%)

### Activity: AI Use-Case Analysis (25 points)
- **Task**: Complete a 1-page concept brief analyzing one AI use-case in your field or area of interest
- **Required Sections**:
  1. Use-case description (2-3 sentences)
  2. Potential benefits (2-3 bullet points)
  3. Potential risks or limitations (2-3 bullet points)
  4. Your assessment: Is this a good fit for AI? Why or why not? (2-3 sentences)
- **Format**: PDF or Word document, 1 page maximum
- **Due**: End of Week 1 (Sunday 11:59 PM)
- **Evaluation Criteria**:
  - Clear use-case description (25%)
  - Thoughtful analysis of benefits and risks (50%)
  - Reasoned assessment with justification (25%)

## Instructor Notes
- **Module 1.1**: Emphasize clear definitions and avoid jargon. Provide visual aids to distinguish approaches. Encourage students to explore tools hands-on.
- **Module 1.2**: Encourage students to think critically about both benefits and risks. Set tone for responsible AI use throughout course.
- **Differentiation**: Provide non-technical and technical examples. Offer optional advanced reading for technical learners.
- **Asynchronous Considerations**: Provide clear instructions for tool access. Create discussion forums that encourage peer interaction. Offer office hours or Q&A forum for questions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
- Ensure captions or transcripts for all videos
- High-contrast slides and materials
- Provide alternative formats for activities
- Allow flexible participation in discussions
- Ensure all tools and resources are accessible
